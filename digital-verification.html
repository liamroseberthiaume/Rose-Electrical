Digital Design & Verification

Everywhere else on this site shows finished work. This page shows work in progress — a deliberate run at digital design and hardware verification, from logic gates through to a UVM testbench for a RISC-V processor core. I'd rather show the build than wait nine months and present the result with no working shown.

Built so far

Digital logic foundations

Combinational and sequential design, Karnaugh minimisation, finite state machines, and the timing that governs all of it — setup and hold constraints, clock skew, metastability, and why a two-flop synchroniser is the standard answer to crossing clock domains.

SystemVerilog RTL

Writing synthesisable RTL rather than code that merely simulates: the three-block FSM idiom, blocking versus non-blocking assignment, and the handful of mistakes that silently infer a latch or produce hardware that behaves differently from the simulation.

FPGA toolchain, end to end

Vivado from RTL to bitstream on an AMD Artix-7 — synthesis, pin constraints, reading a timing report, and getting a design onto real hardware rather than leaving it in a waveform viewer.

Next up

UART transceiver

Transmitter and receiver written from the RS-232 frame spec rather than from a tutorial, verified with a self-checking testbench driven by external test vectors, then synthesised onto the Artix-7 and talking to a terminal over USB. The write-up will include the bugs, not just the result.

Targeting October 2026

Where this is going

A UVM verification environment for an open-source RISC-V core — constrained-random instruction generation biased toward pipeline hazards, lockstep comparison against an instruction-set simulator, and functional coverage closure. That's the destination; everything above is the road to it.

Why this

Making it work once is the easy half

On a modern chip, verification absorbs more engineering effort than design does — and it's a genuinely different discipline. A designer asks whether the thing works. A verification engineer asks what input would prove it doesn't, builds the machinery to go looking, and then has to show the machinery itself is trustworthy.

That's the part I find interesting: a testbench you've never seen fail is a testbench you have no evidence works. So every project on this page gets the same treatment — plant a fault, confirm the checker catches it, and write down what it took to find.

Build log

What's actually happened

September 2026

First design running on real hardware

Vivado installed and working end to end: a design written, synthesised, constrained to the board's actual pins, and programmed onto an Artix-7. Most of the time went into the constraints file rather than the RTL, which was the lesson.

August 2026

Foundations — gates through state machines

Worked through combinational and sequential logic properly: CMOS at transistor level, K-map minimisation with don't-cares, static hazards, and finite state machines built both from a spec and reverse-engineered from a schematic. Finished on timing — setup, hold, clock skew, metastability and synchroniser design.
